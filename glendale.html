Sample brief · Glendale, CA

What Glendale City Hall is deciding next

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 25, 2026 from the City of Glendale's posted Dec 1 City Council and Dec 2 Planning Commission agendas and staff reports on its official PrimeGov meeting portal. Agendas can change; always confirm against the final agenda.

Dec 1, 2026City Council action item (final vote)

1. Water rates jump on Jan 1, 2027: final vote

What's happening: Council closed the public hearing on Nov 17. On Dec 1 it votes on Glendale Water & Power's five-year water rates, starting Jan 1, 2027. The updated rate table shows the monthly fixed charge for a standard 5/8-inch meter going from $19.37 to $38.69. Commercial water moves from one rate of $4.04 per unit to two tiers: $4.00, then $6.71 above your allotment. Apartment buildings go from $2.80 to $3.92 per unit in the first tier.

Why it matters: Restaurants, laundromats, car washes and apartment owners: budget for higher water bills from January. Larger meters pay more. Use the rate table to estimate your own bill.

Source: Updated water rate table, Dec 1, 2026 City Council (item 10a)
Dec 1, 2026City Council public hearing

2. Appeal of a new car wash at 722 N. Pacific Avenue

What's happening: Council hears an appeal against a two-story, 5,878 sq. ft. car wash with 29 parking spaces (26 for self-service vacuums). It would replace the former Acapulco restaurant building at Pacific Avenue and Burchett Street, in the C2 commercial zone. The Design Review Board approved the design 2-1 on Aug 27 with 12 conditions. Staff recommend Council uphold that approval.

Why it matters: Nearby businesses and property owners on N. Pacific Avenue: this decides what replaces a large corner lot, and how traffic enters from Burchett Street. Developers: it shows how Glendale handles appeals on commercial sites.

Source: Staff report, Dec 1, 2026 City Council (item 11a)
Dec 1, 2026City Council action item

3. Digital ad kiosks on Glendale sidewalks

What's happening: Council is asked to start a zoning code change (PZC-0019-2026) to allow digital wayfinding kiosks in the public right-of-way. Today the code bans electronic advertising signs there. The kiosks would show maps, city alerts and local business listings next to paid ads. Staff are finishing a Request for Proposals to pick a vendor that would share ad revenue with the City.

Why it matters: Storefront businesses on busy corridors: kiosks could appear on sidewalks near you, with local business listings. Sign, media and marketing firms: an RFP and new ad space are coming.

Source: Staff report, Dec 1, 2026 City Council (item 10f)
Dec 1, 2026City Council consent item

4. Free energy and water upgrades for small businesses

What's happening: Council votes on a two-year, up to $1.5 million agreement with Synergy Companies. It keeps the home upgrade program running and launches a Business Energy and Water Upgrade Program. Small business customers of Glendale Water & Power would get a no-cost energy and water assessment, a written report and installation of savings measures.

Why it matters: Small business owners: with water rates rising in January, a free assessment and fixtures could cut your bills. Ask GWP when sign-ups open.

Source: Staff report, Dec 1, 2026 City Council (item 5b)
Dec 2, 2026Planning Commission

5. Lot split for two new homes at 1420 Valley View Road

What's happening: The Commission considers splitting a vacant 15,000 sq. ft. lot in the R1 zone into two 7,500 sq. ft. lots. Each would get a new one-story, 2,242 sq. ft. house with a two-car garage. Because a 1908 Craftsman house on the site was demolished, the Final EIR finds a significant impact that can't be avoided. The Commission must certify the EIR and adopt a Statement of Overriding Considerations to approve the map.

Why it matters: Builders and infill developers: this shows how Glendale treats lot splits on sites with a lost historic home. Neighbors can comment at the meeting.

Source: Staff report, Dec 2, 2026 Planning Commission (item 6a)
