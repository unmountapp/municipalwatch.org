Sample brief · Culver City, CA

What Culver City is deciding now

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared October 29, 2026 from the City of Culver City's official agendas and staff documents on Legistar. Items marked "confirm on final agenda" are scheduled but not yet on a posted agenda.

Nov 11, 2026Planning Commission public hearing (confirm on final agenda)

1. Six-story, 40-unit rental building at 4076-4080 Lafayette Place

What's happening: On Oct 28, staff recommended continuing this Site Plan Review (Project P2026-0156-SPR-DOBI) to a Nov 11 hearing. On Oct 22 the City received an appeal of the project's administrative density bonus approval, and the applicant asked that the appeal be heard at the same time. The plan replaces four homes with a six-story (up to 65 ft) building: 40 rental units, including 3 very low-income and 3 moderate-income units, and 44 parking spaces reached from the alley off Braddock Drive.

Why it matters: Neighboring owners, landlords, brokers and developers: this is an early test of how Culver City handles state-law housing projects next to the Lafayette historic district. The staff report notes state law limits the City's ability to deny or condition the project. The Nov 11 agenda is not posted yet.

Source: Oct 28 Planning Commission agenda (item PH-1) Project summary
Oct 26, 2026City Council consent item

2. Downtown Culver City BID: 2027 assessments up for final adoption

What's happening: Council's Oct 26 agenda included final adoption of the ordinance confirming the Downtown Culver City Business Improvement District's 2027 work plan and levy. The staff report says the assessments take effect January 1, 2027. 2027 rates include $463 for retail and fitness up to 1,000 sq ft, $1,629 for restaurants and bars with up to 50 seats, $2,701 for hotels and $111 for independent contractors.

Why it matters: Downtown retailers, restaurants, hotels and theaters: this is your 2027 BID charge, set by business type and size or seat count. Budget for it now. The money funds downtown marketing, decorations and extra maintenance.

Source: Oct 26 City Council agenda (item C-11) Ordinance and 2027 rate table
Oct 26, 2026City Council consent item

3. Bids coming for concrete street and sidewalk work (about $992,500)

What's happening: Council was asked to approve final plans and authorize a notice inviting bids for the Minor Pavement & Concrete Rehabilitation Project (PZ-554). It covers concrete street reconstruction on Harter Ave (Culver Blvd to Wagner St), Moore St at Washington Blvd, several Washington Blvd spots including the westbound approach at Sepulveda, plus the Ocean Drive sidewalk (Overland Ave to Fairbanks). The estimate is $992,500 with contingency. Construction is set to start in spring 2027.

Why it matters: Concrete and paving contractors: watch for the bid notice. Businesses and residents on these blocks: expect lane and sidewalk work next spring.

Source: Oct 26 City Council agenda (item C-4) Project exhibits and plans
Oct 26, 2026City Council action item

4. Faster bidding on City public works projects

What's happening: Council took up the introduction of an ordinance amending Municipal Code Sections 3.02.030 and 3.07.090. It would let the City Engineer and City Traffic Engineer approve plans and specifications, and drop the rule that Council must approve plans before a project goes out to bid. Council would still award construction contracts above the City Manager's purchasing authority.

Why it matters: Contractors who bid Culver City work: projects could reach the street faster, with less notice from Council agendas before bids open. An ordinance needs a second reading at a later meeting before it takes effect; confirm on final agenda.

Source: Oct 26 City Council agenda (item A-1) Proposed ordinance
Oct 26, 2026City Council consent item

5. City letter to every licensed business about a voluntary pledge

What's happening: Council's agenda included a resolution supporting the national Business for Democracy Pledge. It directs staff to send the resolution and a pledge link to every business holding a current business license or business tax certificate, and to promote the pledge for 60 days on City channels.

Why it matters: Every Culver City business may get this mailing. The resolution says signing is fully voluntary and will not affect any license, permit, contract or City service.

Source: Oct 26 City Council agenda (item C-13) Resolution text
