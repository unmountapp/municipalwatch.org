Sample brief · Culver City, CA

What Culver City is deciding now

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared October 22, 2026 from the City of Culver City's official agendas and staff documents on Legistar. Items marked "confirm on final agenda" are scheduled but not yet on a posted agenda.

Oct 26, 2026City Council public hearing (confirm on final agenda)

1. Public hearing on the 2027 Culver City Arts District business assessment

What's happening: On Oct 13, Council adopted a resolution of intent to levy the Arts District Business Improvement District (BID) assessment for 2027 and set a public hearing for Oct 26 at 7:00 PM. Written and oral protests can be made at the hearing. 2026 dues run $375, $250 or $125 a year on Washington Blvd, La Cienega Blvd, National Blvd, Helms Ave and Wesley St, and $281.25, $187.50 or $93.75 on Smiley Dr, Blackwelder St, Adams St, Hines Ave, Fairfax Ave and Perry Dr.

Why it matters: Galleries, creative firms, shops and restaurants in the Arts District: this assessment is billed to your business. Businesses that open during 2027 are assessed on a prorated basis. The Oct 26 agenda is not posted yet, so confirm the item on the final agenda.

Source: Resolution of intent (Oct 13 packet) 2027 work plan and dues
Oct 13, 2026City Council public hearing

2. Downtown Culver City BID: 2027 assessment ordinance introduced

What's happening: Council held the public hearing and introduced an ordinance confirming the Downtown Culver City Business Improvement District's 2027 work plan and levy. The ordinance says there was no majority protest. Listed 2027 rates include $463 for retail and fitness up to 1,000 sq ft, $2,701 for hotels and $1,629 for bars and restaurants with up to 50 seats.

Why it matters: Downtown retailers, restaurants, hotels and theaters: these are your 2027 BID charges, set by business type and size or seat count. The ordinance still needs a second reading before it takes effect.

Source: Oct 13 City Council agenda (item PH-1) Ordinance and 2027 rate table
Oct 13, 2026City Council action item

3. Community Workforce Agreement for City construction contracts

What's happening: Council took action on a Community Workforce Agreement with the Los Angeles/Orange Counties Building and Construction Trades Council. The agenda item sets coverage thresholds of $750,000 for multi-trade contracts and $150,000 for prime specialty contracts.

Why it matters: Contractors who bid City of Culver City public works: projects above these thresholds would be covered by the agreement, which can change hiring, dispatch and labor terms on City jobs. Read the agreement before your next bid.

Source: Oct 13 City Council agenda (item A-1) Agreement text
Oct 14, 2026Planning Commission public hearing

4. New water-conservation and California native planting rules for landscaping

What's happening: The Planning Commission took up Zoning Code Amendment P2026-0170-ZCA, which updates the landscaping chapter (CCMC 17.310) and adds California native planting requirements for new and rehabilitated landscapes. The draft resolution recommends that City Council approve it. Under the draft, state water-efficient landscape rules apply to new landscapes over 500 sq ft and rehabilitated landscapes over 2,500 sq ft. Single-family and two-family projects are generally excepted.

Why it matters: Developers, commercial property owners, landscape designers and contractors: plant palettes and irrigation plans on future Culver City projects may need to change. The amendment still goes to City Council for a final vote.

Source: Oct 14 Planning Commission agenda (item PH-1) Draft resolution and code text
Oct 20, 2026Advisory Committee on Housing and Homelessness

5. Transit-oriented development alternative plan options (SB 79)

What's happening: The committee was scheduled to discuss and give direction on Culver City's Transit Oriented Development alternative plan options. The packet includes an SB 79 zoning map and maps of the alternative plan areas.

Why it matters: Property owners, developers and brokers near Culver City transit stops: SB 79 is the state law allowing denser housing near major transit. The City's alternative plan could change what can be built on your parcel. This is an advisory step; decisions come later at Planning Commission and City Council.

Source: Oct 20 committee agenda (item A-1)
