Sample brief · Culver City, CA

What Culver City is deciding now

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 19, 2026 from the City of Culver City's official Nov 16 City Council agenda, adopted ordinance and staff attachments, and the Nov 19 Bicycle & Pedestrian Advisory Committee agenda on Legistar. Items can change; always confirm against the linked source.

Takes effect Dec 16, 2026City Council ordinance, adopted Nov 16

1. Citywide parking caps for new development take effect Dec 16

What's happening: On Nov 16, Council adopted the ordinance (P2026-0196-ZCA, item A-3) that sets a maximum number of parking spaces for new development and redevelopment. The ordinance takes effect 30 days after adoption. Examples from the rate table: offices and general retail 1 space per 350 sq. ft.; restaurants 1 per 100 sq. ft.; multifamily buildings of 16+ units 2 spaces per unit plus 1 guest space per 4 units. A project can go over the cap by 2 spaces or 5%, whichever is greater, only with an Administrative Use Permit. The ratios are to be re-checked every 3 years.

Why it matters: Developers, architects and commercial owners: this limits how much parking you may build. Check projects that will be filed after Dec 16 against the rate table now.

Source: adopted ordinance and rate table
Nov 16, 2026City Council public hearing

2. Arts District BID assessments continue for 2027

What's happening: After a public hearing (item PH-1), Council found no majority protest and adopted the resolution to levy the Culver City Arts District Business Improvement District assessment for 2027. The district covers Washington Blvd between National Blvd and Fairfax Ave, La Cienega Blvd from Washington to Blackwelder St, and nearby side streets. The work plan's dues schedule has three tiers: $375, $250 or $125 a year on the main streets, and lower rates on side streets. New plans include artist-wrapped utility boxes and bus-shelter art.

Why it matters: Galleries, restaurants, retailers and creative firms in the Arts District: expect a 2027 BID assessment. Each location is assessed separately.

Source: Arts District 2026-2027 work plan and dues schedule
Nov 16, 2026City Council consent calendar

3. Downtown BID agreement extended through 2027

What's happening: Council's consent calendar (item C-3) included extending the City's agreement with the Downtown Business Association, which runs the Downtown Culver City BID, through Dec 31, 2027. The attached 2027 budget projects $441,000 in BID assessment collections. The largest costs are maintenance ($130,000) and event staff ($155,000). The work plan also says the DBA will keep reviewing Downtown parking needs.

Why it matters: Downtown businesses and landlords: this is how your BID dues will be spent in 2027. Confirm the final vote in the meeting minutes.

Source: Downtown BID 2027 work plan and budget
Nov 16, 2026City Council consent calendar

4. $239,000 KPMG study on the 2028 Olympics and local business

What's happening: The consent calendar (item C-2) included an agreement with KPMG, up to $239,000, for an economic and community investment analysis, strategy and implementation plan for Culver City's 2028 Olympic activations. The same agenda included a proclamation naming Nov 28, 2026 as Small Business Saturday.

Why it matters: Hotels, restaurants, retailers and event venues: this study will shape how the City plans for Olympic visitors. Watch for chances to give input. Confirm the final vote in the meeting minutes.

Source: Nov 16 City Council agenda (item C-2)
Nov 19, 2026, 6:00 PMBicycle & Pedestrian Advisory Committee

5. Design options for the next phases of Better Overland

What's happening: The committee receives a report and discusses design options for future phases of the Better Overland Project (item A-2). It also hears a report on technology the City uses to monitor, enforce and improve mobility (item A-1). The meeting is in the Dan Patacchia Meeting Room, City Hall, 1st Floor.

Why it matters: Businesses and property owners on Overland Ave: street design choices can change curb parking, loading and access. This is an advisory committee, so its input goes to staff and Council later.

Source: Nov 19 committee agenda (item A-2)
