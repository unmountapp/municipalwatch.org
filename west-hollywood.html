Sample brief · West Hollywood, CA

What West Hollywood City Hall is deciding next

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 20, 2026 from the City of West Hollywood's official Nov 16 City Council, Nov 19 Planning Commission and Nov 12 Rent Stabilization Commission agendas and staff reports on its Granicus portal. Items marked "confirm on final agenda" are scheduled but not yet on a posted agenda.

Dec 3, 2026Planning Commission public hearing (confirm on final agenda)

1. Nine-unit building at 1280 N. Sweetzer Avenue

What's happening: A new four-story, nine-unit residential building with one affordable unit, over an underground garage, is proposed on a vacant lot. On Nov 19, staff asked the Commission to continue the hearing to Thursday, December 3, 2026, so the applicant can submit more information.

Why it matters: Neighbors, landlords and brokers on the Sweetzer block: new units and construction are coming to the street if approved. Contractors and architects: this is a Housing Accountability Act project, so the city has limited room to say no.

Source: Staff report, Nov 19, 2026 Planning Commission
Dec 7, 2026City Council, possible second reading (confirm on final agenda)

2. Rent Stabilization Ordinance changes for landlords

What's happening: On Nov 16, Council was asked to introduce on first reading an ordinance on tenant habitability plans, property-wide non-compliance, rent adjustment appeal timelines and displacement monitoring, plus matching changes to the Rent Stabilization Regulations. An ordinance needs a second vote; the next regular Council meeting is December 7. The new rules are set to take effect July 1, 2027.

Why it matters: Landlords and property managers of rent-stabilized buildings: work at occupied units will need a Tenant Habitability Plan, and registration and compliance rules tighten. Start planning 2027 renovations with this in mind.

Source: Staff report, Nov 16, 2026 City Council
Nov 12, 2026Rent Stabilization Commission

3. Security deposit interest for 2026: 4.0%

What's happening: Staff recommended the Commission announce a 4.0% annual simple interest rate on security deposits held during 2026. The Commission kept its existing formula (the average of five high-yield online savings accounts) after landlords asked for a change. Interest is paid to tenants, or given as a one-time rent reduction, before February of the next year.

Why it matters: Landlords and property managers: budget for 4.0% interest on every West Hollywood deposit you held in 2026, and pay or credit it before February 2027.

Source: Staff report, Nov 12, 2026 Rent Stabilization Commission
Nov 16, 2026City Council consent calendar

4. Cannabis business tax rebate extended through June 30, 2027

What's happening: Council was asked to adopt an ordinance extending the temporary 2% cannabis business tax rebate through June 30, 2027, for up to 11 eligible businesses. To qualify, a business must be current on city and state cannabis taxes, owe the city nothing, follow all city ordinances, and show significant progress on its social equity commitments.

Why it matters: Licensed cannabis retailers and lounges: this is real money on your tax bill, but only if your paperwork and social equity reporting are in order.

Source: Staff report, Nov 16, 2026 City Council
Nov 19, 2026Planning Commission public hearing

5. ADU rules updated to match new state law

What's happening: The Commission held a hearing on zoning code changes for ADUs and junior ADUs, with a recommendation going to City Council. A new state law, AB 956, requires cities to approve two detached new ADUs on a single-family lot, not one. Except where noted, the new state laws take effect January 1, 2027.

Why it matters: Homeowners, ADU builders and architects: more ADU combinations become by-right in 2027. Watch for the Council vote that puts these rules in the city code.

Source: Staff report, Nov 19, 2026 Planning Commission
