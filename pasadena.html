Sample brief · Pasadena, CA

What Pasadena is deciding now

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared October 22, 2026 from the City of Pasadena's official agendas, agenda packets and Rent Stabilization Department notices. Items marked "confirm on final agenda" are scheduled but not yet on a posted agenda.

Oct 28, 2026Planning Commission (confirm on final agenda)

1. Small-lot subdivisions, SB 9 in historic districts and faster lot splits

What's happening: Code changes to the Subdivision and Zoning Codes were continued from the Oct 14 Planning Commission meeting to Oct 28. They would put SB 684 and SB 1123 in place for small-lot subdivisions of 10 or fewer lots, change SB 9 two-unit standards (including allowing SB 9 in historic districts if no historic structure is affected), and let lot line adjustments and subdivisions of four or fewer lots be approved ministerially.

Why it matters: Builders, small developers and owners of residential lots: this could shorten approval paths for small infill projects and lot splits in Pasadena. The Oct 28 agenda is not posted yet, so confirm the item on the final agenda. The Commission then makes a recommendation; City Council decides later.

Source: Oct 14 Planning Commission agenda (item 3B)
Oct 31, 2026Rent Stabilization Department deadline

2. 2026 rental registration closes October 31

What's happening: Owners of rental property must complete the 2026 Rental Registration by Oct 31. The fee is $236.54 per unit. A seven-day grace period follows, with no late fees through Nov 7. A final walk-in help session is on Thursday, Oct 29, 9 a.m. to 12 p.m., at 199 S. Los Robles Ave.

Why it matters: Landlords and property managers: registration status is tied to rent increase eligibility (see item 3). Picking the wrong exemption type can get a request denied and sent back for correction.

Source: Rent Stabilization Department, October 2026 news
Oct 15, 2026Rental Housing Board special meeting

3. "Substantial compliance" rules that could block rent increases

What's happening: The Rental Housing Board held a second study session on defining "substantial compliance." Staff proposed that a landlord may be found not in substantial compliance, and so unable to take an annual rent increase, if the property is not registered, has outstanding habitability violations or open notices of non-compliance, or a Hearing Officer has found the landlord out of compliance. Landlords would get notice and a chance to cure. The Board was asked whether to direct staff to return with draft language.

Why it matters: Owners and managers of rent-stabilized units: keeping registration current and clearing violations may become a firm condition for your next rent increase. Watch for the draft regulation language at a future Board meeting.

Source: Oct 15 Rental Housing Board agenda packet (item 1)
Oct 15, 2026Rental Housing Board special meeting

4. 2027 security deposit interest rate: 0.05% recommended

What's happening: Staff recommended that the Board adopt a 0.05% interest rate on security deposits for Jan 1 to Dec 31, 2027, based on the average savings rate at 15 banks as of Oct 1, 2026. That works out to $0.50 a year on a $1,000 deposit. The City Charter requires the interest to be paid annually.

Why it matters: Landlords and property managers: update your deposit interest calculations and tenant notices for 2027. Check the Board's final action before you rely on the rate.

Source: Oct 15 Rental Housing Board agenda packet (item 4)
Oct 20, 2026City Council (first reading)

5. Water waste ordinance: no drinking water on non-functional turf at businesses

What's happening: Council held the first reading of an ordinance amending the City's water waste rules (Municipal Code Chapter 13.10). It puts in place state law AB 1572, which bans using potable water to irrigate non-functional turf on commercial, industrial and institutional properties, public agencies and homeowners' association common areas. The state requires local rules and customer notice by Jan 1, 2027. A second reading is still needed.

Why it matters: Commercial and industrial property owners, HOAs and landscape contractors: decorative lawns watered with drinking water will need to be replaced or switched to other water sources. Plan any turf conversion and irrigation changes now.

Source: Oct 20 City Council agenda (item 9) Ordinance fact sheet
