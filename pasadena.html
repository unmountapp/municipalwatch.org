Sample brief · Pasadena, CA

What Pasadena is deciding now

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 18, 2026 from the City of Pasadena's official agendas, staff reports, bid notices and adopted ordinances. Items marked "confirm on final agenda" are scheduled but not yet on a posted agenda.

Nov 18, 2026Planning Commission special meeting

1. Data centers: allowed only at colleges and hospitals

What's happening: At 6:30 p.m. in the Permit Center Hearing Room (175 N. Garfield Ave.), the Planning Commission holds a public hearing on a Zoning Code change. It would define data centers as a land use and allow them only with a conditional use permit, as an ancillary use to traditional college campuses and hospitals that have a Master Plan in the PS (Public, Semi-Public) zone. The Commission recommends; City Council decides later.

Why it matters: Commercial property owners, brokers and tech tenants: as proposed, a data center would not be an option on ordinary commercial or industrial sites. The same agenda has a study session on safe parking sites and a briefing on how the city reviews density bonus concessions and waivers.

Source: Nov 18 Planning Commission agenda (item 4A) Staff report
Nov 19, 2026Design Commission · preliminary consultation

2. 289 apartments to replace a big-box store at 452 N. Los Robles Ave.

What's happening: The Design Commission gives early feedback on a plan to demolish a 1965 big-box retail building and its 154-space surface lot. In its place: a six-to-seven story building with 289 apartments and 439 parking spaces. The project uses the state density bonus, with 44 affordable units (22 very low income, 22 moderate income). No vote is taken at a preliminary consultation.

Why it matters: Nearby businesses, landlords and brokers on Los Robles, Villa St. and Oakland Ave.: this is a major change to the block, with years of demolition and construction ahead if it moves forward. Meeting starts at 6:30 p.m.

Source: Nov 19 Design Commission agenda (item 4A) Staff report
Nov 19, 2026Design Commission · final design review

3. 46-unit apartment building at 511 S. Oak Knoll Ave.

What's happening: The Design Commission takes up final design review of a new four-story, 47,360 sq ft building with 46 apartments and 67 underground parking spaces on a vacant lot. It includes affordable units and uses the state density bonus, with one concession raising the height limit from 40 feet to 47 feet, 10 inches. Staff recommend approval with conditions.

Why it matters: Developers and architects: final design approval is one of the last steps before building permits, and this case shows how Pasadena handles density bonus height concessions in its "City of Gardens" multi-family rules.

Source: Nov 19 Design Commission agenda (item 5A) Staff report
Dec 3, 2026Public Works bid deadline

4. Bids due on the Holly Street Bridge seismic retrofit

What's happening: Pasadena Public Works is taking sealed bids until 2:00 p.m. Thursday, Dec 3, for the Holly Street Bridge Seismic Retrofit & Rehabilitation Project (Project ID 2026-IFB-CIV-0063). Work includes a seismic retrofit, deck treatment, concrete and girder repair, and new ADA curb ramps and sidewalk at Linda Vista Ave. and Holly St. Questions are due by 2:00 p.m. Nov 19. Bidders need an active Class A license.

Why it matters: General engineering and concrete contractors: this is federally funded (FHWA Highway Bridge Program, administered by Caltrans), so federal-aid and prevailing wage rules apply. Bids go through the city's OpenGov portal.

Source: Notice Inviting Bids, Holly Street Bridge
Adopted Oct 27, 2026City Council ordinance

5. No more drinking water on decorative lawns at businesses

What's happening: Ordinance 7482 adds the state's nonfunctional turf rule (AB 1572) to Pasadena's water waste code. Using drinking water to irrigate nonfunctional turf is banned on commercial, industrial and institutional properties starting Jan 1, 2028, and on HOA and common-interest common areas starting Jan 1, 2029. City-owned and other public properties start Jan 1, 2027. Watering needed to keep trees healthy is still allowed.

Why it matters: Commercial property owners, HOAs and landscapers: decorative grass on these properties has to be replaced or switched off potable water by the deadline. Plan and budget the conversion now.

Source: Ordinance 7482 (Oct 27, 2026)
