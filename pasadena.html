Sample brief · Pasadena, CA

What Pasadena is deciding now

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 4, 2026 from the City of Pasadena's official agendas, staff reports, public hearing notices and Rent Stabilization Department news. Items marked "confirm on final agenda" are scheduled but not yet on a posted agenda.

Nov 4, 2026Hearing Officer public hearing

1. Two rental buildings ask to convert to condos

What's happening: The Hearing Officer hears two condominium conversions. One is a five-unit rental building at 830 Brooks Ave. (Tentative Tract Map #85005). The other is a four-unit rental building at 390 Cliff Dr. (Tentative Parcel Map #84208). Both would let units that are now rented be sold one by one. Staff recommend approval with conditions.

Why it matters: Small apartment owners and brokers: this is the path to selling units one at a time. The staff report says the owner must meet parking, utility meter and warranty rules, and give tenants relocation help under the city's Housing Charter.

Source: Nov 4 Hearing Officer agenda (items A and B) Staff report (830 Brooks)
Nov 5, 2026Design Commission

2. 143-unit affordable project on East Colorado Blvd.

What's happening: The Design Commission has a six-story, 100% affordable housing project at 2006-2030 E. Colorado Blvd. and 25 S. San Marino Ave. on its agenda. It would replace auto service buildings with 143 homes plus a manager's unit and 64 parking spaces. It uses the state density bonus with five concessions and one waiver. Staff recommend continuing the hearing to Dec 3, 2026.

Why it matters: East Colorado businesses and nearby owners: this is a big change to the block, with fewer parking spaces than homes. The same agenda also has a redesigned parking garage facade at 3468-3484 E. Foothill Blvd.

Source: Nov 5 Design Commission agenda (item 5A)
Nov 8, 2026Rent Stabilization Department deadline

3. Late fees on rental registration start November 8

What's happening: The 2026 Rental Registration deadline was Oct 31. The grace period ends Nov 7. Starting Nov 8, unpaid fees get a late fee, starting at 15% of the $236.54 per-unit Rental Housing Fee. The late fee grows the longer it stays unpaid. The Rental Housing Board adopted a 0.05% security deposit interest rate for 2027, payable by Jan 31, 2028.

Why it matters: Landlords and property managers: finish any open registrations this week. Separately, 2026 deposit interest (0.12%) is due to tenants by Jan 31, 2027.

Source: Rent Stabilization Department, November 2026 news Deposit interest notice
Nov 10, 2026City Council public hearing · confirm on final agenda

4. Appeal of a new 4,955 sq ft home at 3140 E. California Blvd.

What's happening: On Oct 27 the Council set a Nov 10, 6:00 p.m. public hearing on an appeal of the Board of Zoning Appeals' approval of Single-Family Compatibility Permit No. 7337. The permit allows a new two-story, 4,955 square-foot house at 3140 E. California Blvd.

Why it matters: Builders, architects and homeowners planning larger houses: this shows how far Pasadena's compatibility review can go, including appeals to Council after the Board of Zoning Appeals approves a project.

Source: Oct 27 City Council agenda (public hearing set)
Nov 18, 2026Planning Commission special meeting · confirm on final agenda

5. New zoning rules for data centers

What's happening: The Planning Commission holds a public hearing at 6:30 p.m. at the Hale Building on Zoning Code changes that would define data centers as a land use. They would be allowed only with a conditional use permit, for certain institutional uses with a Master Plan. The change is citywide. The Commission recommends; City Council decides at a separate hearing. The agenda will be posted by Nov 13.

Why it matters: Commercial property owners, brokers and tech tenants: as written in the notice, data centers would not be an option on ordinary commercial sites. Written comments go to commentsPC@cityofpasadena.net before the meeting.

Source: Public hearing notice, data center regulations
