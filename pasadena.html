Sample brief · Pasadena, CA

What Pasadena is deciding now

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared October 28, 2026 from the City of Pasadena's official agendas, agenda packets and Rent Stabilization Department notices. Items marked "confirm on final agenda" are scheduled but not yet on a posted agenda.

Oct 28, 2026Planning Commission public hearing

1. Smaller, more flexible work/live units

What's happening: Staff recommend changing the Zoning Code and Specific Plans to ease rules for work/live units. The minimum unit size would drop from 1,250 to 800 square feet. The living area could grow from 30% (max 400 sq ft) to 50% (max 800 sq ft). Parking would be a flat 1.5 spaces per unit everywhere, instead of 3 spaces per 1,000 sq ft outside certain Specific Plans.

Why it matters: Owners of commercial and mixed-use buildings, small developers and creative businesses: smaller work/live units with less parking could make conversions and new projects pencil out. The Commission recommends; City Council makes the final decision later.

Source: Oct 28 Planning Commission agenda (item 3A) Staff report
Oct 31, 2026Rent Stabilization Department deadline

2. 2026 rental registration closes October 31

What's happening: Owners of rental property must complete the 2026 Rental Registration by Oct 31. The fee is $236.54 per unit. A seven-day grace period follows, with no late fees through Nov 7. The last walk-in help session is Thursday, Oct 29, 9 a.m. to 12 p.m., at 199 S. Los Robles Ave.

Why it matters: Landlords and property managers: register now to avoid late fees. Picking the wrong exemption type (property vs. unit) can get a request denied and sent back for correction.

Source: Rent Stabilization Department, October 2026 news
Oct 27, 2026City Council (second reading)

3. Turf watering ban: dates for businesses and HOAs

What's happening: Council was set to adopt the water waste ordinance on second reading. It bans using drinking water to irrigate non-functional turf. The ban starts Jan 1, 2028 for commercial, industrial and most institutional properties, and Jan 1, 2029 for HOA and common-interest common areas. Watering needed to keep trees and other perennial non-turf plants healthy is still allowed.

Why it matters: Commercial property owners, HOAs and landscape contractors: decorative lawns watered with drinking water will need to be replaced or switched to another water source. Budget for turf conversion now. Check the Oct 27 minutes to confirm final adoption.

Source: Oct 27 City Council agenda (item 8) Ordinance text
Nov 10, 2026City Council public hearing

4. Appeal of a new 4,955 sq ft home at 3140 E. California Blvd.

What's happening: On Oct 27 the Council set a Nov 10, 6:00 p.m. public hearing on an appeal of the Board of Zoning Appeals' approval of Single-Family Compatibility Permit No. 7337. The permit allows a new two-story, 4,955 square-foot house at 3140 E. California Blvd.

Why it matters: Builders, architects and homeowners planning larger houses: this shows how far Pasadena's compatibility review can go, including appeals to Council after the Board of Zoning Appeals approves a project.

Source: Oct 27 City Council agenda (item 7c, public hearing set)
Jan 31, 2027Rent Stabilization Department deadline

5. Pay 2026 security deposit interest (0.12%) by January 31

What's happening: Landlords of rent-stabilized units must pay tenants interest on security deposits held at least one year. The rate for Jan 1 to Dec 31, 2026 is 0.12%, due by Jan 31, 2027. A $1,000 deposit earns $1.20. Payment can be by check, rent credit, or electronic payment if both sides agree in writing.

Why it matters: Landlords and property managers: add this to your January tasks. The Rental Housing Board sets the 2027 rate separately, and that rate applies to interest paid in January 2028.

Source: Rent Stabilization Department, security deposit notice
