Sample brief · Long Beach, CA

What Long Beach City Hall is deciding next

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 24, 2026 from the City of Long Beach's official Dec 1, 2026 City Council agenda and staff reports on its PrimeGov portal. Items can change; always confirm against the final agenda.

Dec 1, 2026City Council, regular agenda, 5:00 PM

1. Late-night rules, fees and paid parking for bars and restaurants in Belmont Shore and Downtown

What's happening: Staff return with options on late-night public safety near businesses that serve alcohol. Options include a Belmont Shore district with stricter rules for businesses open after 10 PM and more after midnight, extra fees on business licenses in Belmont Shore or Downtown, and a $250 citywide fee on alcohol sellers to fund an alcohol coordinator. Staff also suggest adding business hours to business license records, an Entertainment Permit for late-night alcohol service on Second Street, and paid parking on Second Street until 2 AM (it is free after 7 PM today).

Why it matters: Bars, restaurants and landlords on Second Street and in Downtown: Council direction here could mean new fees, new operating rules and later parking charges for your customers.

Source: City Council staff report, Dec 1, 2026 (item 18)
Dec 1, 2026City Council public hearing, 5:00 PM

2. Housing Omnibus zoning changes, including a path to permit existing informal ADUs

What's happening: Council holds the hearing and first reading on the citywide Housing Omnibus Package. It sets up a permit process for existing informal ADUs, sets a 60-day review goal for some housing and shelter projects, raises Site Plan Review thresholds, lets owners maintain and rebuild existing unit density that exceeds current zoning, and allows interim housing and some health care uses in more places. It also needs Coastal Commission review for the Coastal Zone.

Why it matters: Landlords with unpermitted units, ADU builders, architects and developers: these rule changes can affect what you can legalize or build and how long review takes.

Source: City Council staff report, Dec 1, 2026 (item 15)
Dec 1, 2026City Council, regular agenda, 5:00 PM

3. Possible ban or limits on large data centers, and new rules for battery storage sites

What's happening: Councilmembers Kerr and Ricks-Oddie ask staff to start General Plan and zoning amendments to prohibit or control new large-scale commercial data centers, and to write siting and fire safety rules for Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS), using LA County's rules as a reference. Staff would report back within 120 days, including on any pending applications and possible interim actions.

Why it matters: Industrial property owners, brokers, developers and energy firms: what you can build on industrial land in Long Beach could change, and interim limits are on the table.

Source: City Council staff report, Dec 1, 2026 (item 17)
Dec 1, 2026City Council, consent calendar, 5:00 PM

4. First step to form an Enhanced Infrastructure Financing District (about 2,711 acres)

What's happening: Council is asked to adopt a Resolution of Intention to form an EIFD and its Public Financing Authority. The proposed area covers about 2,711 acres, or 8.4% of the city. Staff say it does not raise property taxes; it redirects part of future property tax growth to fund public facilities. The County Board of Supervisors is expected to consider partnering on Dec 8. The first public hearing is tentatively set for June 10, 2027.

Why it matters: Property owners and developers inside the proposed boundary: future public investment may be steered to your area, and you will get mailed notice of hearings.

Source: City Council staff report, Dec 1, 2026 (item 10)
Dec 1, 2026City Council, regular agenda, 5:00 PM

5. $2.4M for emergency dredging to put sand back on the Peninsula

What's happening: Public Works asks Council to approve an agreement with the Harbor Department and $2.4 million from Tidelands emergency reserves. Sand dredged from the Pier J Turning Basin would be placed along the Peninsula between 54th Place and 72nd Place, where storms exposed the bulkhead and damaged the boardwalk. Under the emergency permit the work must be done by April 30, 2027.

Why it matters: Peninsula homeowners, property managers and marine contractors: expect beach work and equipment along the shore this winter and spring.

Source: City Council staff report, Dec 1, 2026 (item 24) Full Dec 1 agenda
