Sample brief · Long Beach, CA

What Long Beach City Hall is deciding next

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 10, 2026 from the City of Long Beach's official agendas (PrimeGov), staff reports and public hearing notices. Items can change; always confirm against the final agenda.

Nov 17, 2026City Council public hearing, 5:00 PM

1. Fast-track emergency permits for storm repairs in the Coastal Zone

What's happening: Council will hold a hearing on an emergency ordinance (ZCA26-003) that lets the City's Zoning Administrator issue emergency coastal development permits in the parts of the Coastal Zone the City controls (Districts 1, 2 and 3). Today the City has no such process, so emergency work must go to the California Coastal Commission or wait for a noticed public hearing. The emergency permit would be free, but a regular coastal permit, with the usual fees, would still be required later. The Coastal Commission keeps control of emergency permits in its own state jurisdiction.

Why it matters: Coastal property owners, property managers and contractors in Naples, the Peninsula, Belmont Shore and Downtown: storm repairs this El Niño season could be approved locally and much faster. Know the new application steps before you need them.

Source: City Council staff report, Nov 17, 2026 (item 22)
Nov 17, 2026City Council, consent calendar, 5:00 PM

2. Full-liquor license transfer for Kelly's Steakhouse, 5716 E. 2nd St.

What's happening: Martin & Mlouk Hospitality Group LLC, dba Kelly's Steakhouse, applied for a Type 47 (on-sale general, eating place) license transfer in Belmont Shore. Police list possible grounds for protest: over-concentration (the census tract allows 3 licenses and has 14), homes within 100 feet and a hospital within 600 feet. Staff recommend filing a protest with the state ABC and withdrawing it if a Conditional Use Permit Exemption is granted.

Why it matters: Restaurants, bars and landlords on 2nd Street: this shows how the City handles new or transferred liquor licenses in an area that is already over the limit. The state's response is due after Nov 18.

Source: City Council staff report, Nov 17, 2026 (item 21) Info sheet
Nov 17, 2026City Council, ordinance, 5:00 PM

3. $750,000 cap lifted for storm-emergency job order contracts

What's happening: Public Works asks Council to adopt an urgency ordinance that temporarily suspends the $750,000 single job order limit in the City's Job Order Contract program. It applies only to projects needed for the emergency from Hurricane Marie and the 2026-27 El Niño storm season, such as Peninsula bulkhead and Naples seawall repairs. It would take effect immediately. Prevailing wage and Project Labor Agreement rules still apply where required.

Why it matters: Contractors: larger emergency repair jobs can go straight to the City's pre-qualified job order contractors instead of a separate bid. Subcontractors and suppliers should know who holds those contracts.

Source: City Council staff report, Nov 17, 2026 (item 33)
Nov 17, 2026City Council, 5:00 PM

4. Shelter crisis declaration and temporary shelter sites from Dec 1

What's happening: Council will consider declaring a shelter crisis, which suspends some local rules, including zoning provisions in the Municipal Code. It would let the City Manager pick one or more temporary shelter sites for one year starting Dec 1, 2026, while the shelter at 702 W. Anaheim St. is renovated. That shelter has 81 year-round beds and 40 winter beds, run by PATH.

Why it matters: Property owners and businesses: temporary shelter sites could be chosen without the usual zoning process. Owners of vacant buildings may also be approached as possible sites.

Source: City Council staff report, Nov 17, 2026 (item 28)
Dec 1, 2026City Council public hearing (confirm on final agenda)

5. Citywide zoning changes in the Omnibus Housing Ordinance

What's happening: The Nov 17 Council agenda lists a hearing reminder: on Tuesday, Dec 1, Council is set to consider a Zoning Code Amendment and Local Coastal Program Amendment changing the zoning rules for the Omnibus Housing Ordinance (citywide). The staff report is not posted yet. Separately on Nov 17, Council will vote to reapply for the State's Prohousing Designation, which expires in January 2027.

Why it matters: Developers, architects and landlords planning housing: citywide zoning rule changes can affect what you can build and how fast. Read the staff report as soon as it posts.

Source: City Council agenda, Nov 17, 2026 (hearing reminder) Prohousing staff report (item 24)
