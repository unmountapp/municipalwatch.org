Sample brief · Los Angeles, CA

What LA City Hall is deciding this week

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 16, 2026 from the official City of Los Angeles City Council agendas for Nov 17 and Nov 18, 2026. Items can be continued or changed; always confirm against the linked source.

Nov 18, 2026City Council, 10:00 a.m. (Item 38, Council File 26-1564)

1. Citywide: no more drinking water on decorative lawns, plus a 3-day watering limit

What's happening: Council takes first vote on amendments to the Emergency Water Conservation Ordinance. Using drinking water on “non-functional” (decorative) turf would be banned: for commercial, industrial and institutional customers from Jan 1, 2028, and for HOAs and common interest developments from Jan 1, 2029. Phase I would also limit landscape watering to 3 days a week and require a shut-off nozzle on hoses. Properties in disadvantaged communities can get more time.

Why it matters: Commercial and multifamily owners, HOAs, property managers and landscapers: budget now for turf replacement and irrigation changes. Fines are billed on the water bill, and repeat violations can lead to restricted service.

Source: LA City Council agenda, Nov 18, 2026 (Item 38)
Nov 17, 2026City Council, 10:00 a.m. (Item 7, Council File 26-1321)

2. Citywide: cannabis licenses can be renewed for 2027 despite unpaid city taxes

What's happening: Council is asked to adopt an ordinance letting a licensed cannabis business file its 2027 renewal (window Sept 1 to Nov 30, 2026) even if it owes city business tax. The business must still file a timely, complete application and pay all renewal fees. The agenda ties this to a Cannabis Tax Amnesty Program.

Why it matters: Dispensaries, cultivators and distributors with a tax balance: this could keep your license active while you resolve what you owe. The renewal window closes Nov 30, so act early.

Source: LA City Council agenda, Nov 17, 2026 (Item 7)
Nov 17, 2026City Council, 10:00 a.m. (Item 17, Council File 16-0149-S3, Council District 3)

3. Woodland Hills: $3.6 million for Ventura Boulevard sidewalks and street work

What's happening: Council is asked to move about $3.6 million ($2,546,560 from the Village at Westfield Topanga Public Benefits Trust Fund, plus $1,050,000 in Council District 3 funds) to sidewalk repairs and street improvements for the Reimagine Ventura Boulevard Streetscape Project, Phase II, Royer Avenue to Sale Avenue.

Why it matters: Shops, restaurants and landlords on that stretch of Ventura: expect construction on sidewalks and curbs. Plan for access, parking and signage during the work, and a better streetscape after.

Source: LA City Council agenda, Nov 17, 2026 (Item 17)
Nov 17, 2026City Council, 10:00 a.m. (Item 3, Council File 26-1207, Council District 13)

4. Silver Lake: appeal of a 76-unit addition at 2413–2499 N. Silver Lake Blvd

What's happening: PLUM recommends that Council deny a CEQA appeal and uphold the City Planning Commission approval. The project adds three buildings (four and five stories) with 76 new units, six for very low-income households, to an existing 48-unit complex, for 124 units total. It includes 119 underground parking spaces and about 35,600 cubic yards of soil export. Council’s time limit to act is Nov 20.

Why it matters: Nearby owners, tenants and businesses: expect months of construction and truck trips if the approval stands. Developers: this is a test of how Council handles CEQA appeals on infill housing.

Source: LA City Council agenda, Nov 17, 2026 (Item 3)
Nov 18, 2026City Council, 10:00 a.m. (Item 28, Council File 26-0088-S2)

5. Citywide: Measure ULA “mansion tax” exemption for some affordable housing deals

What's happening: Council takes first vote on two ordinances. One would exempt from the ULA transfer tax sales to two-tier partnerships and LLCs whose general partner or managing member is a nonprofit with affordable housing development or management experience. The other makes technical changes to the covenants required by the ULA program.

Why it matters: Owners selling larger properties, brokers and affordable housing developers: a sale to a qualifying nonprofit-led buyer may avoid the ULA tax. That can change the price a buyer can offer.

Source: LA City Council agenda, Nov 18, 2026 (Item 28)
