Sample brief · Los Angeles, CA

What LA City Hall is deciding in the next two weeks

This is the format of a Municipal Watch weekly brief: each item, its date, what it means for your business, and a link to the official source.

Sample prepared November 9, 2026 from official City of Los Angeles City Council and PLUM Committee agendas and the City Planning Commission agenda. The City Council meeting on Nov 11 is cancelled. Items can be continued or changed; always confirm against the linked source.

Nov 10, 2026City Council, 10:00 a.m. (Item 35, Council File 09-0969-S4)

1. Citywide: higher fees to appeal a planning decision you did not apply for

What's happening: Council is asked to raise the fee for appeals filed by someone other than the applicant. Neighbors inside the notice radius would pay $1,145. People outside the radius would pay $2,289. The City Attorney would then draft the ordinance.

Why it matters: Developers and applicants: a higher fee may mean fewer appeals that delay approved projects. Neighbors, businesses and community groups: challenging a nearby project would cost more.

Source: LA City Council agenda, Nov 10, 2026 (Item 35)
Nov 10, 2026City Council, 10:00 a.m. (Items 7–8, Council Files 26-0742 and 22-0600-S54)

2. Citywide: stormwater (LID) rules and fees for building projects

What's happening: Council is asked to have the City Attorney draft a way to release pending projects with 500 to 2,499 sq ft of hard surface from the old Low Impact Development rules. Those projects would follow the current ordinance’s thresholds instead. A second item keeps the current LID ordinance and adopts revised LID development service fees.

Why it matters: Homeowners, ADU builders and small contractors with projects stuck under the old rules could save design and construction costs. Every builder should check the new LID plan-check fees before budgeting.

Source: LA City Council agenda, Nov 10, 2026 (Items 7 and 8)
Nov 10, 2026Planning and Land Use Management (PLUM) Committee, 2:00 p.m. (Item 4, Council File 26-1355, Council District 15)

3. San Pedro: every parcel rezoned under the new Zoning Code

What's happening: The committee takes up the San Pedro Zoning Code Update. It would rezone all parcels in the San Pedro Community Plan Area to the City’s New Zoning Code. It also repeals the San Pedro CPIO overlay, amends the San Pedro Specific Plan, and adopts new maps, including an Alcohol Permission Area Map. The committee’s recommendation then goes to the full Council.

Why it matters: San Pedro property owners, landlords, brokers and businesses: what you can build, and where alcohol sales are allowed, may change on your parcel. Check the new zone for your address.

Source: PLUM Committee agenda, Nov 10, 2026 (Item 4)
Nov 10, 2026PLUM Committee, 2:00 p.m. (Item 7, Council File 26-0906-S10, Council District 14)

4. Boyle Heights: proposed freeze on permits for large cold storage warehouses

What's happening: City Planning brings an interim control ordinance. It would stop construction, remodel, change-of-use and occupancy permits, and utility connections, for large cold storage warehousing and distribution uses. It covers all light industrial, industrial and hybrid industrial land in the Boyle Heights Community Plan area. The item follows the fire at 1400 S Los Palos St.

Why it matters: Industrial owners, logistics and food-distribution tenants, and brokers in Boyle Heights: a cold storage lease, sale or build-out could stall while the ordinance is in effect.

Source: PLUM Committee agenda, Nov 10, 2026 (Item 7)
Nov 12, 2026City Planning Commission, after 8:30 a.m. (Items 6–8, CPC-2025-4421-CA, CPC-2025-4430-CA, CPC-2026-4383-CA)

5. Citywide: “Missing Middle LA” rules for ADUs, lot splits and starter homes

What's happening: The commission holds a public hearing on three code changes. They update ADU rules and let qualifying ADUs be sold separately as condos (AB 1033). They write SB 9 two-unit and lot-split rules into the code. They create a Starter Home Bonus Program, update Small Lot Subdivision rules, and add “Lot Amenity Space” standards for projects under six units. The commission’s recommendation then goes to the City Council.

Why it matters: ADU builders, small developers, homeowners and agents: these rules set what you can build and sell on single-family and small lots across the City. Selling an ADU on its own could change project math.

Source: City Planning Commission agenda, Nov 12, 2026 (Items 6, 7 and 8)
